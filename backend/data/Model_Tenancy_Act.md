# The Model Tenancy Act, 2021

An Act to establish Rent Authority to regulate renting of premises and to protect the interests of landlords and tenants and to provide speedy adjudication mechanism for resolution of disputes and matters connected therewith or incidental thereto.

---

## Chapter I — Preliminary

### Section 1. Short title, extent and commencement
1. This Act may be called the (Name of State/Union territory) Tenancy Act, 2020.
2. It extends to the whole of the State/Union territory of (Name of State/Union territory).
3. It shall come into force on such date as the State Government/Union territory Administration may, by notification in the Official Gazette, appoint and different dates may be appointed for different provisions of this Act.

### Section 2. Definitions
In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires —

- **(a) "landlord"** — whether called landowner or lessor or by any other name, means a person who receives or is entitled to receive the rent of any premises, on his own account, if the premises were let to a tenant, and includes:
  - (i) his successor-in-interest; and
  - (ii) a trustee or guardian or receiver receiving rent for any premises, or entitled to so receive, on account of or on behalf of or for the benefit of any other person such as a minor or person of unsound mind who cannot enter into a contract.
- **(b) "local authority"** — means a Village Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti, Zila Parishad, Municipal Corporation, Municipal Council, Nagar Panchayat, Planning or Development Authority (by whatever name called), the Cantonment Board, or a civil area committee appointed under section 47 of the Cantonment Act, 2006, or such other body entitled to function as a local authority in any city or town.
- **(c) "notification"** — means a notification published in the Official Gazette of the State or Union territory; "notify" is construed accordingly.
- **(d) "premises"** — means any building or part of a building let, or intended to be let, on rent for residential or commercial use — **excluding** hotels, lodging houses, dharamshalas, inns, and industrial use — but **including**:
  - (i) garden, garage or closed parking area, vacant land, grounds and out-houses appertaining to the building; and
  - (ii) any fitting to the building for its more beneficial enjoyment.
- **(e) "prescribed"** — means prescribed by rules made by the State Government/Union territory Administration under this Act.
- **(f) "property manager"** — means a person or legal entity, including a rental agent, authorised by the landlord to manage the premises and represent the landlord in dealings with the tenant.
- **(g) "rental agent"** — means any person who negotiates or acts on behalf of landlord or tenant (or both) in a rental transaction and receives remuneration/fees/charges for the service — includes property dealers, brokers, or middlemen by whatever name called.
- **(h) "Rent Authority"** — an officer appointed under section 30.
- **(i) "Rent Court"** — constituted under section 33.
- **(j) "Rent payable"** — the rent as specified in section 8.
- **(k) "Rent Tribunal"** — constituted under section 34.
- **(l) "Schedule"** — a schedule annexed to this Act.
- **(m) "Sub-tenant"** — a person to whom the tenant sublets whole or part of the premises, or to whom the tenant transfers/assigns rights under the tenancy agreement, via a supplementary agreement to the existing tenancy agreement.
- **(n) "Tenant"** — whether called lessee or by any other name, means a person by/on account of/on behalf of whom rent is payable to the landlord under a tenancy agreement; includes a sub-tenant and a person continuing in possession after termination of tenancy (before or after commencement of this Act); **excludes** any person against whom an eviction order/decree has been made.

### Section 3. Act not to apply to certain premises
1. Nothing in this Act applies to:
   - (a) premises owned/promoted by the Central/State Government, Union territory Administration, local authority, Government undertaking/enterprise, statutory body, or Cantonment Board;
   - (b) premises owned by a company, university, or organisation and let to its employees as part of a service contract;
   - (c) premises owned by religious or charitable institutions as notified by the State/Union territory;
   - (d) premises owned by auqafs registered under the Waqf Act, 1995, or by any trust registered under applicable public trust law;
   - (e) other buildings/categories specifically exempted in the public interest by notification.
2. Notwithstanding (1), if owner and tenant of an exempted premises agree that their tenancy be regulated under this Act, the landlord may inform the Rent Authority of that agreement when informing of the tenancy agreement under section 4.

## Chapter II — Tenancy

### Section 4. Tenancy agreement
1. No person may let or take on rent any premises after commencement of this Act except by a written agreement, which must be informed to the Rent Authority jointly by landlord and tenant, in the form in the First Schedule, within **two months** of the date of the tenancy agreement.
2. If landlord and tenant fail to jointly inform the Rent Authority, each must separately inform it within **one month** of expiry of the period in (1).
3. The Rent Authority shall, within three months of its appointment, put in place a digital platform (in local vernacular/State-UT language) for such submissions.
4. On receiving the information (with First Schedule documents), the Rent Authority shall, within **seven working days**:
   - (a) provide a unique identification number to the parties; and
   - (b) upload tenancy agreement details on its website in the local vernacular/State-UT language.
5. Terms of authorisation of a property manager (if any) shall be as agreed between landlord and tenant in the tenancy agreement.
6. Information provided under (1)/(2) is conclusive proof of tenancy facts; absent such information, landlord and tenant are not entitled to relief under this Act.

### Section 5. Period of tenancy
1. Every tenancy after commencement of this Act is valid for the period agreed and specified in the tenancy agreement.
2. The tenant may request renewal/extension within the agreed period; if the landlord agrees, a new tenancy agreement may be entered into.
3. If a fixed-term tenancy ends without renewal and the tenant fails to vacate, the tenant is liable to pay enhanced rent under section 23.
   - *Proviso:* if the tenancy term expires during a **force majeure** event affecting the locality, the landlord must, on the tenant's request, allow the tenant to continue in possession (on the same terms) until one month after the disastrous event ceases.
   - *Explanation:* "force majeure" means war, flood, drought, fire, cyclone, earthquake, or other natural calamity affecting habitation.

### Section 6. Rights and obligations of successor in case of death
Terms of the tenancy agreement bind the successors of a deceased landlord or tenant, who have the same rights and obligations for the remaining tenancy period.

### Section 7. Restriction on subletting
1. After commencement of this Act, a tenant shall not — except via a supplementary agreement to the existing tenancy agreement —
   - (a) sub-let whole or part of the premises; or
   - (b) transfer or assign rights under the tenancy agreement.
2. Where premises are sub-let via such supplementary agreement, landlord and tenant must jointly inform the Rent Authority within **two months** of its execution, in the First Schedule form.

## Chapter III — Rent

### Section 8. Rent payable
Rent payable is the rent agreed between landlord and tenant per the tenancy agreement.

### Section 9. Revision of rent
1. Rent revision shall be per the terms of the tenancy agreement.
2. Where the landlord, after commencement of tenancy, has (with prior written agreement) incurred expenditure on improvement/addition/structural alteration (excluding section 15 repairs), the landlord may increase rent by an agreed amount, effective one month after work completion.

### Section 10. Rent Authority to determine revised rent in case of dispute
On application by landlord or tenant, the Rent Authority may determine the revised rent/charges and fix the effective date.

### Section 11. Security deposit
1. The security deposit shall be as agreed in the tenancy agreement, and shall:
   - (a) **not exceed two months' rent** for residential premises; and
   - (b) **not exceed six months' rent** for non-residential premises.
2. The deposit shall be refunded on the date of taking vacant possession, after due deduction of the tenant's liabilities.

## Chapter IV — Rights and Obligations of Landlord and Tenant

### Section 12. Original tenancy agreement to be retained by landlord and tenant
The agreement shall be signed in duplicate; each party retains one original signed copy.

### Section 13. Rent and other charges payable and receipt for payment thereof
1. Tenant shall pay rent/charges within the agreed period.
2. Landlord (or property manager) shall, on receipt of payment, provide a signed receipt forthwith against acknowledgement.
   - *Proviso:* electronic payment bank acknowledgment is conclusive proof of payment.

### Section 14. Deposit of rent with Rent Authority
1. If the landlord refuses to accept rent/charges or refuses to give a receipt, the tenant shall pay by postal money order (or other prescribed method) for two consecutive months; if the landlord still refuses, the tenant may deposit the amount with the Rent Authority.
2. If the tenant cannot determine to whom rent is payable, the tenant may similarly deposit with the Rent Authority.
3. The Rent Authority shall enquire and pass appropriate orders on such deposits.
4. Withdrawal of deposited rent does not by itself amount to an admission against the landlord (or any tenant claim), if withdrawn to the extent of agreed rent.

### Section 15. Repair and maintenance of property
1. Landlord and tenant shall keep the premises in as good condition as at tenancy commencement (excepting normal wear and tear), per responsibilities in the Second Schedule or the agreement.
2. Responsibility for shared common facilities shall be as specified in the tenancy agreement.
3. If the tenant fails/refuses to carry out their repairs, the landlord may carry them out (or remove tenant-erected structures without consent) and deduct the cost from the security deposit; tenant must pay any excess within one month of notice.
4. If the landlord refuses to carry out their repairs, the tenant may do so and deduct the cost from future rent — **capped at 50% of one month's rent per month**.
5. If premises become uninhabitable and the landlord refuses required repairs after written request, the tenant may abandon the premises on **15 days' written notice**.
6. If premises become uninhabitable due to force majeure, the landlord cannot charge rent until restoration.
   - *Proviso:* if the landlord fails to restore habitability under (5) or (6), the security deposit and advance rent must be refunded within 15 days of notice-period expiry, after due deductions.
   - *Explanation:* "force majeure" defined as in section 5.

### Section 16. Tenant to look after premises
The tenant shall: (a) not intentionally/negligently damage the premises; (b) inform the landlord in writing of any damage; (c) take reasonable care of the premises, fittings and fixtures, keeping it reasonably habitable.

### Section 17. Entry into premises
1. Landlord/property manager may enter after **at least 24 hours' notice** (written or electronic) for: (a) repairs/replacement/work; (b) habitability inspection; (c) other reasonable cause specified in the agreement.
2. Notice must specify day, time, and reason.
   - *Proviso:* no entry before sunrise or after sunset.
   - *Further proviso:* no notice required in emergencies (war, flood, fire, cyclone, earthquake, other natural calamity).

### Section 18. Information as to property manager
If a property manager is engaged, the landlord must provide the tenant: (a) manager's name; (b) proof of authorisation; (c) specific purposes and period of authorisation; (d) if a legal entity, the entity's name and authorised contact person.

### Section 19. Duties of property manager and consequences of violation
1. Duties include: (a) collecting rent against receipt; (b) essential repairs on landlord's behalf; (c) periodic inspection; (d) giving notices to tenant (maintenance, rent delay, rent revision, vacation, renewal); (e) helping resolve disputes; (f) other matters only on landlord's instructions.
2. If the property manager contravenes (1) or acts against landlord's instructions, the Rent Authority may remove the manager or impose compensatory costs.

### Section 20. Withholding essential supply or service
1. No landlord/property manager shall withhold essential supply/service to the tenant.
2. On tenant application, the Rent Authority may pass an interim order for immediate restoration, pending inquiry.
3. Inquiry shall be completed within **one month** of the application.
4. The Rent Authority may award compensation up to **two months' rent**.
5. The Rent Authority may levy a penalty up to **twice the monthly rent** on the tenant if the application is found frivolous/vexatious.
   - *Explanation:* essential services include water, electricity, piped cooking gas, passage/lift/staircase lighting, conservancy, parking, communication links, sanitary services, and security fixtures/features.

## Chapter V — Eviction and Recovery of Possession of Premises by the Landlord

### Section 21. Eviction and recovery of possession of premises by landlord
1. A tenant shall not be evicted during the tenancy except by written agreement, or per (2), or per section 22.
2. The Rent Court may order eviction on application by the landlord, on grounds including:
   - (a) tenant does not agree to pay rent under section 8;
   - (b) tenant has not paid arrears for **two consecutive months** (with interest) within one month of a demand notice under the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, s.106(4);
   - (c) tenant parted with possession (whole/part) without landlord's written consent;
   - (d) tenant continued to misuse premises after notice to desist (misuse = encroachment, public nuisance, property damage, harm to landlord's interest, or immoral/illegal purposes);
   - (e) landlord needs premises vacated for repair/construction/rebuilding/addition/alteration/demolition not otherwise possible (tenant may reoccupy only per specified conditions);
   - (f) premises required for repairs/construction/etc. due to change of land use by competent authority;
   - (g) tenant gave notice to vacate and landlord, relying on it, contracted to sell or took other steps whose interests would suffer;
   - (h) tenant made structural change/erected permanent structure without landlord's written consent.
3. No eviction under (2)(b) if tenant pays/deposits arrears (with interest) within one month of the demand notice.
4. This relief under (3) is not available again if the tenant defaults for two consecutive months again within the same year.
5. In (2)(e) proceedings, the Rent Court may order eviction from only part of the premises if the landlord agrees.

### Section 22. Eviction and recovery of possession in case of death of landlord
1. On the landlord's death, legal heirs with a bonafide requirement for the premises may apply to the Rent Court for eviction/possession.
2. The Rent Court, if satisfied of bonafide requirement, may order the tenant to hand over vacant possession to the legal heirs.

### Section 23. Enhancement of rent in case of refusal by tenant to vacate
A tenant who fails to vacate on expiry/termination of tenancy is liable to pay:
- (a) **twice** the monthly rent for the first two months; and
- (b) **four times** the monthly rent thereafter, until vacating.

### Section 24. Refund of advance rent by landlord
1. Where the landlord recovers possession under s.21(2) or s.22 and had received advance rent/payment, the landlord must refund it (after deducting dues) before recovery of possession.
2. Failure to refund attracts simple interest at the prescribed rate.

### Section 25. Payment of rent during eviction proceedings
Where the tenant contests eviction (on grounds other than s.21(2)(a)/(b)), the landlord may apply to the Rent Court to direct the tenant to pay rent and other dues (with penal charges) during proceedings.

### Section 26. Permission to build additional structures
1. Tenant shall not carry out structural changes/erect permanent structures without landlord's written consent.
2. If the landlord wants to improve/add structures and the tenant refuses, the landlord may apply to the Rent Court.
3. The Rent Court may permit the work if satisfied the landlord is ready and willing, subject to the improvement not diminishing housing services or causing undue hardship to the tenant. The tenant is responsible for removing any unauthorised structure they built.

### Section 27. Special provision regarding vacant land
1. Where let premises include vacant land on which construction is permissible under municipal bye-laws, and the landlord cannot obtain possession from the tenant, the landlord (or legal heirs) may apply to the Rent Court.
2. If satisfied, the Rent Court may: (a) direct severance; (b) place the landlord in possession of the vacant land; (c) determine rent for the remaining premises; (d) make other appropriate orders.

### Section 28. Vacant possession to landlord
Where a landlord's (or legal heirs') interest is determined and the Rent Court orders recovery of possession, the order binds all occupants, who must give vacant possession.

### Section 29. Provisions regarding notice of giving up possession by tenant
A tenant may give up possession on the notice required by the tenancy agreement; absent such a stipulation, **at least one month's** written notice is required.

## Chapter VI — Rent Authorities, Their Powers and Appeals

### Section 30. Rent Authority
The District Collector/Magistrate, with State/UT Government approval, appoints an officer (not below Deputy Collector rank) as Rent Authority within their jurisdiction.

### Section 31. Power and procedure of Rent Authority
The Rent Authority has all powers of a Rent Court for proceedings under sections 4, 9, 10, 14, 15, 19, or 20, following the procedure in sections 35–36.

### Section 32. Appeals
1. A person aggrieved by a Rent Authority order may appeal to the Rent Court with territorial jurisdiction.
2. The appeal must be filed within **30 days** of the order.

---

## Chapter VII — Rent Courts and Rent Tribunals

### Section 33. Rent Court
The District Collector/Magistrate, with State/UT approval, appoints an Additional Collector/Additional District Magistrate (or equivalent) as Rent Court.

### Section 34. Rent Tribunal
The State/UT Government, in consultation with the jurisdictional High Court, may appoint a District Judge/Additional District Judge as Rent Tribunal for each district.

### Section 35. Procedure to be followed in Rent Court and Rent Tribunal
1. The Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 does not apply (except as provided); procedure follows natural justice:
   - (a) application/appeal filed with affidavit and documents;
   - (b) notice issued to opposite party with copies;
   - (c) opposite party files reply with affidavit/documents;
   - (d) applicant may file rejoinder;
   - (e) hearing date fixed; summary inquiry as needed.
2. Cases should be disposed of within **60 days** (reasons recorded in writing if not).
3. Witness evidence given by affidavit (cross-examination may be ordered if needed in the interest of justice).
4. CPC provisions on service of summons apply mutatis mutandis.
5. Applications/appeals in prescribed form.
6. Maximum **three adjournments** per party, with recorded reasons and possible cost order.
7. Applications under s.21(2)(a),(b),(e),(f),(g) or s.22 decided within **90 days**.
8. Applications under s.21(2)(c),(d) decided within **30 days**.

### Section 36. Powers of Rent Court and Rent Tribunal
1. Same powers as a civil court under CPC 1908 for: summoning/examining witnesses, discovery/production of documents, commissions for witness examination or local investigation, receiving affidavit evidence, dismissing/deciding ex-parte, setting aside ex-parte orders, execution of orders without reference to civil court, review, revision, and other prescribed matters.
2. Proceedings deemed judicial proceedings under IPC ss.193, 228, 196; Rent Court/Tribunal deemed civil courts for CrPC ss.195 and Chapter XXVI purposes.
3. For inquiries: may (a) enter/inspect premises with 24 hours' notice, between sunrise and sunset; (b) require production of documents.
4. May appoint assessors/valuers with special knowledge.
5. May correct clerical/arithmetical mistakes or accidental omissions in its orders.
6. May exercise powers of a Judicial Magistrate (first class) for fine recovery under CrPC.
7. Orders executable as a civil court decree.
8. May set aside ex-parte orders on satisfactory cause shown (improper service or sufficient cause for non-appearance).
9. Save as expressly provided, orders are final (subject to appeal) and not questionable in any other proceeding.

### Section 37. Appeal to Rent Tribunal
1. Appeal against a Rent Court order lies to the jurisdictional Rent Tribunal within **30 days**, with a certified copy of the order.
2. Rent Tribunal serves notice, fixes hearing within 30 days of service, and disposes the appeal within **60 days** of that hearing date.
3. Additional documents allowed at any stage if necessary for a just decision (not more than once).
4. Interlocutory orders may be passed during pendency.
5. On deciding, the Tribunal may (with recorded reasons) confirm, set aside, or modify the Rent Court's order.

### Section 38. Execution of order
1. The Rent Court executes orders (its own, the Tribunal's, or others under this Act) by: (a) delivering possession; (b) attaching bank accounts to recover amounts; (c) appointing an advocate or competent person/officer for execution.
2. The Rent Court may seek help from local government/body/police (applicant bears any police-assistance costs as decided by the Court).
3. Execution proceedings conducted summarily; applications disposed of within **30 days** of notice on the opposite party.

## Chapter VIII — Miscellaneous

### Section 39. Officers and other employees
The State/UT Government, in consultation with the Rent Authority/Court/Tribunal, may determine and appoint necessary officers/employees.

### Section 40. Jurisdiction of civil courts barred in certain matters
1. No civil court shall entertain suits/proceedings covered by this Act (save as otherwise provided).
2. The Rent Court's jurisdiction is limited to tenancy agreements submitted under the First Schedule and does not extend to questions of title/ownership.

### Section 41. Court fees
1. The Court Fees Act, 1870 applies to applications/appeals before the Rent Authority/Court/Tribunal.
2. For fee computation, recovery-of-possession applications and Tribunal appeal memoranda are deemed suits between landlord and tenant.
3. Fees before the Rent Authority equal those for an interlocutory application in a civil court.

### Section 42. Members etc. to be public servants
Every member of the Rent Authority, Rent Court, and Rent Tribunal is deemed a public servant under IPC section 21.

### Section 43. Protection of action taken in good faith
No suit/prosecution/legal proceeding lies against any person or the Rent Authority/Court/Tribunal for anything done in good faith under this Act.

### Section 44. Power to make rules
1. The State/UT Government may make rules (subject to previous publication) to carry out this Act.
2. Rules may cover, among other things: the digital platform (s.4(3)); manner of rent payment/deposit (s.14); applications for recovery of possession (ss.21(2), 22(1)); interest rate for refund failures (s.24(2)); applications for vacant-land building possession (s.27(1)); application/appeal forms (s.35(5)); other matters under s.36(1)(k); execution manner (s.38(1)); and any other prescribed matter.

### Section 45. Laying of rules
Every rule made shall be laid before the State/UT Legislature as soon as may be after being made.

### Section 46. Power to remove difficulties
1. If any difficulty arises in giving effect to this Act, the State/UT Government may, by Gazette order (not inconsistent with the Act), remove the difficulty — **no such order after two years from commencement**.
2. Every such order shall be laid before the State/UT Legislature.

### Section 47. Repeal and savings
1. The State/UT Rent Control Act in force immediately before this Act's commencement is repealed.
2. Pending cases/proceedings under the repealed Act continue to be disposed of under that Act, as if it had continued in force.

## First Schedule — Form for Information of Tenancy
*(See section 4(1))*

Submitted to the Rent Authority, containing:

1. Name and address of the landlord
2. Name and address of the Property Manager (if any)
3. Name(s) and address of the tenant, including email/contact details
4. Description of previous tenancy, if any
5. Description of premises let, including appurtenant land (if any)
6. Date from which possession is given to the tenant
7. Rent payable (per section 8)
8. Furniture and other equipment provided to the tenant
9. Other charges payable: (a) electricity; (b) water; (c) extra furnishing/fittings/fixtures; (d) other services
10. Tenancy/lease/rent agreement (attached)
11. Duration of tenancy
12. PAN of landlord
13. Aadhaar number of landlord
14. Mobile number & email of landlord (if available)
15. PAN of tenant
16. Aadhaar number of tenant
17. Mobile number & email of tenant
18. PAN of Property Manager (if any)
19. Aadhaar number of Property Manager (if any)
20. Mobile number & email of Property Manager (if any)

Enclosures: (1) Tenancy Agreement; (2) self-attested PAN and Aadhaar copies of landlord; (3) self-attested PAN and Aadhaar copies of tenant. Signed by landlord and tenant, with photographs.

## Second Schedule — Division of Maintenance Responsibility Between Landlord and Tenants
*(See section 15(1))*

Unless otherwise agreed in the tenancy agreement: **Part A** items are the landlord's responsibility; **Part B** items are the tenant's responsibility.

**Part A — Responsibilities of the Landlord**
1. Structural repairs (except damage caused by the tenant)
2. Whitewashing of walls and painting of doors and windows
3. Changing and plumbing pipes when necessary
4. Internal and external electrical wiring and related maintenance when necessary

**Part B — Periodic repairs to be got done by the tenant**
1. Changing of tap washers and taps
2. Drain cleaning
3. Water closet repairs
4. Wash basin repairs
5. Bath tub repairs
6. Geyser repairs
7. Circuit breaker repairs
8. Switches and socket repairs
9. Repairs and replacement of electrical equipment (except major internal/external wiring changes)
10. Kitchen fixtures repairs
11. Replacement of knobs and locks of doors, cupboards, windows, etc.
12. Replacement of fly-nets
13. Replacement of glass panels in windows, doors, etc.
14. Maintenance of gardens and open spaces let out to or used by the tenant
